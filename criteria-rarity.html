Heritage Criterion

Rarity

The place is an uncommon surviving example of a local building type, use, feature, landscape or memory.

What this criterion covers

Rarity should be considered in a clear local context. A feature can be common nationally but unusual in the town or district, or it may be one of few surviving examples of something that was once widespread locally.

Examples of useful evidence

  • Local surveys, directories, inventories or historic maps.
  • Comparisons with similar places and their survival.
  • Evidence that a use, technique or tradition has disappeared locally.
  • Photographs showing the uncommon surviving feature.

Consider when nominating

  • Rare compared with what area and time period?
  • How many similar examples are known locally?
  • Which surviving characteristics make this example distinctive?
  • Can the comparison be supported by sources?

Community heritage criterion

Rarity is one way to explain local value; it is not a claim of official designation.
