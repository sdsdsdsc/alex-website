Community heritage criteria

Criteria

These criteria help explain why a place is locally valued. A place does not need to meet every criterion to be worth nominating.

How to use the criteria

The criteria are a simple guide for describing local significance. They are inspired by Local Heritage Listing practice, but they are not official statutory designation criteria.

Choose the criteria that best fit the place and explain the evidence behind them. One strong reason can be enough for a useful community heritage record.

The criteria

Historic interest
The place is connected to local events, industries, people, memories, or patterns of development.
Social or communal value
The place matters to people because of shared use, memory, identity, gathering, care, or community life.
Landmark or streetscape value
The place helps people recognise, navigate, or understand a street, neighbourhood, route, or landscape.
Architectural, design or artistic interest
The place has distinctive design, materials, craftsmanship, public art, or visual character.
Archaeological or evidential interest
The place may hold physical evidence about past activity, settlement, industry, landscape, or community life.
Rarity
The place is an uncommon surviving example of a local building type, use, feature, landscape, or memory.
Group value
The place gains significance because it forms part of a wider group, route, landscape, street, or story.
Age
The place is valued because of its age, survival, long use, or ability to show change over time.
Condition or vulnerability
The place may need attention because it is fragile, changing, at risk, or important to record now.

Important note

These criteria support a community heritage record project. They do not create official listed status or planning authority designation.
