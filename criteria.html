Criteria

Choose an Asset Type to describe what kind of place it is, then use the Heritage Criteria to explain why the place matters locally.

Two parts of a community heritage record

Heritage Criteria: why does this place matter?

Criteria describe the stories, design, evidence, community value, rarity or relationships that give a place local significance.

Asset Type: what kind of place is it?

Asset Type groups a place by its physical character or use so visitors can browse similar community records.

A place may meet several Heritage Criteria, but it normally has one main Asset Type. These are community heritage tools, not official statutory designation tests.

Heritage Criteria

Historic interestConnections to local events, people, industries and change. Social or communal valueShared use, memory, identity, care and community life. Landmark or streetscape valueHow a place shapes local character and orientation. Architectural, design or artistic interestDistinctive design, materials, craft or artistic character. Archaeological or evidential interestPhysical evidence that can help explain the past. RarityAn uncommon local survival, use, feature or memory. Group valueSignificance gained through a wider group, route or story. AgeLong survival, continuing use or evidence of change over time. Condition or vulnerabilityWhy a fragile, changing or at-risk place matters to record now.

Community heritage, not statutory designation

These pages help people prepare consistent community heritage records. They do not create official listed status, legal protection or a planning authority designation.
