Heritage Criterion

Historic interest

The place is connected to local events, industries, people, memories or patterns of development.

What this criterion covers

Historic interest can come from a well-evidenced association or from the way a place helps explain how an area developed and changed. The connection should be meaningful to the local story, not simply a fact that could apply almost anywhere.

Examples of useful evidence

  • Maps, directories, photographs, newspapers or archive records.
  • Information about local work, transport, housing, education or public life.
  • Recorded memories supported by clear names, dates and context.
  • Visible features that help connect the surviving place to its history.

Consider when nominating

  • Which local story does the place help people understand?
  • How strong and specific is the connection?
  • What survives today that makes the history legible?
  • Which sources can another person check?

Community heritage criterion

Historic interest here supports a community record. It is not an official statutory designation test.
