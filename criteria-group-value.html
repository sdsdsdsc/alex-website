Heritage Criterion

Group value

The place gains significance because it forms part of a wider group, route, landscape, street or story.

What this criterion covers

Group value can come from a shared design, use, history, setting or functional relationship. The places do not have to be identical, but the connection should help explain them more clearly together than in isolation.

Examples of useful evidence

  • Maps and photographs showing the complete group and its setting.
  • Shared dates, designers, owners, uses or construction phases.
  • Evidence of a route, planned layout or working relationship.
  • Related community records or nominations that document other parts.

Consider when nominating

  • Which places form the group and where are its limits?
  • What relationship links them?
  • Does the group remain understandable today?
  • Should connected places be nominated or cross-referenced together?

Community heritage criterion

Group value here structures a community record and is not an official designation of the whole group.
