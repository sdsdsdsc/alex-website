About Local Heritage Records

Alex's Photo Board documents community-valued places, local stories, and open heritage data so people can understand what matters locally and why.

What this project records

The site records community-valued places and local heritage assets. These may include public spaces, buildings, parks, streets, routes, landmarks, public art, industrial heritage, and places connected to local memory.

The main public collection is Community Heritage: locally contributed records that have been reviewed and published by this project.

Community and Official Heritage

Community Heritage records describe places people value locally. They are contributed and maintained through this project and do not create statutory designation or legal protection.

Official Heritage records come from national, provincial or municipal registers. They are shown as a separate optional Map layer, with the official designation level and source identified in each record summary.

An official record and its displayed map location can have different sources. Some locations are project-reviewed reference Points, and one is a generalized reference Point showing a documented vicinity. The Map popup states the location type, uncertainty and any important limitation; a reference Point is not automatically an official coordinate, surveyed extent or legal protection boundary.

What a community heritage record means

A community heritage record describes a place and explains why it matters locally. A record may include its location, asset type, local significance, heritage criteria, condition, community use, source references, and related stories.

Bringing factual information together with local meaning helps visitors understand how a place contributes to community identity and memory.

Our community heritage criteria

The project currently uses the following criteria to structure local significance and supporting evidence:

  • Historic interest
  • Social or communal value
  • Landmark or streetscape value
  • Architectural, design or artistic interest
  • Archaeological or evidential interest
  • Rarity
  • Group value
  • Age
  • Condition or vulnerability

These are community heritage criteria, not official statutory designation criteria. They provide a consistent way to describe local significance and the evidence that supports it.

How places and stories connect

Place records can link to related news and history articles, while articles can link back to the places they discuss. These two-way links create a connected set of heritage information that lets visitors move between records and local stories.

Open heritage data

The Open Data page generates heritage.json, which exports public records in a structured format that can help other websites and research tools reuse the data.

The export currently focuses on Community Heritage records, Community News, and Local History Stories.

Public nominations

The Nominate a Community Place page lets visitors suggest community-valued places for admin review.

Public submissions remain separate from published records and must not directly overwrite them.

Important note

Alex's Photo Board is a community heritage record project inspired by Local Heritage Listing practice. It is not an official statutory Local Heritage List and does not represent a planning authority.
