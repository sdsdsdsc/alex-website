Heritage Criterion

Landmark or streetscape value

The place helps people recognise, navigate or understand a street, neighbourhood, route or landscape.

What this criterion covers

A place may be important because it is prominent, marks an entrance or junction, frames a view, anchors a street or contributes strongly to local character. Value can come from a relationship with the surroundings as well as from the feature itself.

Examples of useful evidence

  • Street views showing the place in its wider setting.
  • Historic and current maps that explain routes or changes.
  • Accounts of how people use the feature for orientation or identity.
  • Photographs showing significant views, corners, skylines or groups.

Consider when nominating

  • From where is the place noticed or recognised?
  • What would be harder to understand if it were lost?
  • Does it contribute alone or as part of a group?
  • Which views best demonstrate its local role?

Community heritage criterion

Landmark or streetscape value here describes local significance; it is not an official planning designation.
