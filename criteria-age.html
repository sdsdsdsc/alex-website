Heritage Criterion

Age

The place is valued because of its age, survival, long use or ability to show change over time.

What this criterion covers

Age can help explain significance when a place survives from an earlier phase of local development or contains layers that make change visible. Age alone is rarely enough; connect it to what remains and why that survival matters locally.

Examples of useful evidence

  • Dated maps, photographs, directories, plans or inscriptions.
  • Construction details or materials associated with a period.
  • Records of alterations, rebuilding and continuing use.
  • Comparisons with other surviving places of similar date.

Consider when nominating

  • What is the best-supported date or sequence of dates?
  • Which early features survive?
  • How does the place show continuity or change?
  • Why is its survival meaningful in the local context?

Community heritage criterion

Age helps explain local significance but does not automatically create official heritage status.
