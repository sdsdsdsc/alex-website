Heritage Criterion

Social or communal value

The place matters because of shared use, memory, identity, gathering, care or community life.

What this criterion covers

Value may come from long-standing use, regular gatherings, shared traditions or a strong sense of belonging. A place can remain important even when its original use has changed, provided the present community connection is explained.

Examples of useful evidence

  • Accounts from people who use, maintain or remember the place.
  • Records of clubs, celebrations, worship, markets, protests or mutual aid.
  • Photographs, programmes, notices or local publications.
  • Evidence of continuing care, volunteering or shared responsibility.

Consider when nominating

  • Which community or communities value the place?
  • How is that value expressed in use, memory or care?
  • Is the connection continuing, historic or both?
  • Whose voices and evidence can support the nomination?

Community heritage criterion

Social or communal value here supports a community record. It does not by itself create official status or legal protection.
