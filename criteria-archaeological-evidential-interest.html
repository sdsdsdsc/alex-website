Heritage Criterion

Archaeological or evidential interest

The place may hold physical evidence about past activity, settlement, industry, landscape or community life.

What this criterion covers

Evidence may survive above ground, below ground, within a building or across a landscape. A nomination should distinguish known evidence from reasonable potential and avoid claiming discoveries that have not been demonstrated.

Examples of useful evidence

  • Documented finds, surveys, reports or earlier investigations.
  • Earthworks, foundations, machinery, surfaces or construction phases.
  • Historic maps, aerial images or photographs showing change.
  • Careful field observations recorded without disturbing the place.

Consider when nominating

  • What physical evidence is known to survive?
  • What questions could that evidence help answer?
  • Which claims are confirmed and which remain possible?
  • Could publishing a precise location create a risk to a sensitive site?

Record responsibly

Do not disturb archaeological material or disclose sensitive information. This community criterion is not an official archaeological designation.
