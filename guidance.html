Nomination guidance

Guidance

Prepare a clear nomination by describing the place, explaining why it matters, and adding evidence that helps an admin review it.

What kind of place can be nominated

You can nominate community-valued places such as public spaces, buildings, parks, streets, routes, landmarks, public art, industrial heritage, archaeological features, landscapes, or places connected to local memory.

The place does not need to be grand or famous. It should have a clear local value that can be explained.

What information to provide

Give the place name, address or location description, asset type, area or neighbourhood, and a short explanation of its local significance.

Choose the criteria that fit and explain how the evidence supports them. A strong nomination is clear about both the place and the reason it matters.

What evidence helps

Evidence can include photos, memories, dates, sources, maps, archive references, local stories, community use, or observations about condition and vulnerability.

If you provide a photo or evidence URL, include a caption and source credit where possible.

How map coordinates help

Map coordinates help reviewers identify the exact place. You can start from the heritage map and use the map nomination entry point to pass latitude and longitude into the nomination form.

If you do not know the coordinates, a clear address, nearby streets, landmarks, or a careful location description can still help.

What happens after submission

Nominations are reviewed before publication. A submitted nomination is stored separately from published Community Heritage records and does not automatically become an official or public record.

An admin may approve, reject, request more information, or promote an approved nomination into a published Community Heritage record.

What information is private

Your email is used only for admin follow-up and is not displayed publicly. Admin notes, assessment fields, and review history remain admin-side.

Do not include sensitive personal information in public-facing nomination descriptions, evidence captions, or source notes.

Project position

Alex's Photo Board is building a community heritage record. It is not an official statutory local list.
